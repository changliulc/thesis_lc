Roadside vehicle sensing for intelligent transportation requires continuous acquisition of key information, including vehicle-passage events, vehicle categories, and parking and occupancy states, under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and long-term online operation. Compared with conventional sensing approaches such as video, radar, and inductive loops, a single geomagnetic sensor is compact, inexpensive, easy to deploy in the pavement, and less sensitive to illumination and occlusion, making it suitable for distributed long-term roadside monitoring. However, the information available from a single geomagnetic node is inherently limited. In addition, slow background magnetic drift, vehicle-speed variation, continuous traffic interference, and pseudo-stable states further increase the difficulty of upper-layer task recognition. To address these issues, this thesis takes continuous 50 Hz observations from a single roadside triaxial geomagnetic sensor as the research object. It first investigates vehicle event extraction from continuous triaxial geomagnetic sequences and its system implementation. By constructing a vehicle detection algorithm based on triaxial first-order differences, smoothing, probability fusion, and finite-state-machine constraints, stable extraction of event start and end boundaries is achieved. A vehicle information detection system composed of a geomagnetic detection module, a communication base station, and a cloud platform is also designed, providing a unified event-level input for subsequent vehicle type classification and parking-occupancy inference.

On the one hand, for three-class vehicle type classification under the single-geomagnetic setting, an event-based classification dataset is constructed, and studies are conducted from two perspectives: edge-side lightweight deployment and offline enhanced analysis. The edge-side method extracts statistical features that characterize disturbance amplitude, energy, area, duration, and morphology, and establishes a lightweight Softmax-based baseline through feature-importance ranking, feature de-redundancy, and validation-set search. To address temporal stretching and local misalignment caused by speed variation, the offline method introduces dynamic time warping and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network for enhanced classification. Experimental results show that the edge-side method meets low-overhead deployment requirements, while the offline enhanced method further improves classification performance and mitigates confusion between medium-sized vehicles and adjacent classes.

On the other hand, for parking and occupancy detection in open-road scenarios with complex conditions such as slow background drift, continuous traffic flow, and pseudo-stable states, a dynamic-reference decision method based on signal stable points is proposed. Stable points are extracted via dual criteria over stable windows, and both environmental reference stable points and occupancy reference stable points are constructed. Combined with drift magnitude, similarity gating, an outer finite-state machine, and a degradation branch for continuous traffic, the proposed method enables unified detection of parking confirmation, occupancy maintenance, and release boundaries. Experimental results indicate that the method maintains stable performance under normal conditions and demonstrates good robustness under challenging conditions such as continuous traffic and slow drift.

In summary, this thesis investigates roadside vehicle sensing under the single-geomagnetic-sensor setting, covering vehicle event extraction, three-class vehicle type classification, and parking-occupancy detection. The results suggest that, under constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and easy deployment, a single geomagnetic sensor can provide effective support for roadside vehicle type recognition and parking-occupancy state sensing, offering methodological reference for practical system deployment and functional extension.
