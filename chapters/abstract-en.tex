Roadside vehicle sensing for intelligent transportation systems requires continuous acquisition of vehicle-passage events, vehicle categories, and parking or occupancy states under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and long-term online operation. Compared with conventional sensing approaches such as video, radar, and inductive loops, a single geomagnetic sensor is compact, inexpensive, easy to deploy in the pavement, and less sensitive to illumination and occlusion. These characteristics make it suitable for large-scale distributed roadside monitoring. However, the information available from a single geomagnetic node is inherently limited. In addition, slow background drift, vehicle-speed variation, continuous traffic interference, and pseudo-stable states further increase the difficulty of event detection, vehicle type classification, and parking or occupancy inference. To address these issues, this thesis focuses on a single roadside triaxial geomagnetic sensor and, on the basis of a unified event-level input representation, mainly studies methods for three-class vehicle type classification and for parking/occupancy detection. As a prerequisite, the thesis first organizes vehicle-event extraction from continuous triaxial geomagnetic sequences together with the corresponding system pipeline, thereby establishing a unified event-level input representation and providing consistent data organization and triggering interfaces for subsequent upper-layer tasks.

First, for vehicle-event extraction from continuous triaxial geomagnetic sequences, the basic principle of geomagnetic vehicle detection and its system implementation are studied. A vehicle detection algorithm based on triaxial first-order difference features, smoothing, probability fusion, and finite-state-machine constraints is developed to stably determine the start and end boundaries of geomagnetic disturbance events. Meanwhile, a vehicle information detection system composed of a geomagnetic detection node, a communication base station, and a cloud platform is designed.

Second, for vehicle type classification under the single-geomagnetic-sensor setting, an event-based dataset is constructed and two complementary schemes are developed, namely an edge-deployable lightweight scheme and an offline enhanced classification scheme. In the edge scheme, statistical features that characterize amplitude, energy, area, duration, and waveform morphology are extracted, and a lightweight baseline based on a Softmax classifier is established through random-forest importance ranking, feature de-redundancy, and validation-based search. In the offline scheme, dynamic time warping and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network are introduced to address temporal stretching and local misalignment caused by vehicle-speed variation. Experimental results demonstrate that the edge scheme is suitable for low-overhead deployment, while the offline enhanced method can further improve classification performance and effectively reduce the confusion between medium-sized vehicles and adjacent classes.

Finally, for parking and occupancy detection in open-road scenarios, where slow drift, continuous traffic flow, and pseudo-stable states are common, a dynamic-reference method based on signal stable points is proposed. Stable points are extracted using a dual-criterion stable-window strategy, and both environmental reference points and occupancy reference points are maintained dynamically. Combined with drift magnitude, similarity gating, an outer finite-state machine, and a degradation branch for continuous-traffic conditions, the proposed method realizes unified detection of parking confirmation, occupancy maintenance, and release boundaries. Experimental results show that the proposed method maintains stable performance under normal conditions and provides better robustness under challenging conditions such as continuous traffic and slow background drift.

In summary, this thesis establishes a complete research chain for roadside vehicle sensing under the single-geomagnetic-sensor setting, spanning from vehicle event detection to two upper-layer tasks, namely vehicle type classification and parking or occupancy detection. The results verify that a single triaxial geomagnetic node can still provide effective support for roadside vehicle event detection, vehicle classification, and parking or occupancy management under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and easy deployment.
