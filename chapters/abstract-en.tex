With the sustained growth of urban traffic and the continuous development of smart roads, roadside vehicle sensing has become an important foundation for traffic organization optimization, parking governance, and road space management. For long-term online deployment scenarios, a single geomagnetic sensor offers practical advantages such as low cost, low power consumption, and easy deployment. However, the observation information available from a single geomagnetic node is limited, and it is readily affected by slow background magnetic drift, vehicle-speed variation, continuous traffic interference, and pseudo-stable states. To address these issues, this thesis investigates continuous 50 Hz observations from a single roadside triaxial geomagnetic sensor and develops a technical pipeline from continuous observations to unified event-level inputs and then to high-level task recognition, covering vehicle event extraction, vehicle type classification, and parking-occupancy state determination.

First, for the online extraction of vehicle disturbance events from continuous triaxial geomagnetic sequences, a vehicle event detection method composed of first-order difference feature extraction, three-axis probability fusion, and finite-state-machine constraints is investigated. The proposed method achieves stable extraction of vehicle entry and exit boundaries and forms unified event boundaries, event segments, and their durations, thus providing a shared event-level input basis for subsequent vehicle type classification and parking-occupancy determination.

Second, to address the limited explicit information available for vehicle type recognition under a single geomagnetic sensor setting, as well as the coexistence of temporal stretching and local misalignment of waveforms under natural traffic flow, a vehicle event dataset based on real road scenarios is constructed, and three-class vehicle classification is studied from two perspectives: lightweight edge-side deployment and offline enhanced analysis. On the edge side, statistical features describing disturbance amplitude, energy, area, duration, and morphological distribution are extracted, and a lightweight classification method based on Softmax regression is established. In the offline enhancement stage, a multi-template time-alignment strategy based on dynamic time warping is introduced and combined with a one-dimensional convolutional neural network for enhanced classification. Experimental results show that the edge-side scheme satisfies low-overhead deployment requirements, while the offline enhancement scheme further improves vehicle type discriminability, especially by alleviating the confusion between medium-sized vehicles and adjacent classes.

Finally, to address the difficulty of parking and occupancy determination in open-road scenarios caused by slow environmental drift, continuous traffic flow, and pseudo-stable states, a parking-occupancy detection method based on signal stable points and dynamic reference maintenance is proposed. The method extracts stable points using dual criteria based on intra-window fluctuation and inter-window level difference, constructs environmental and occupancy references, and combines similarity gating, a state maintenance and transition mechanism, and a degradation branch for continuous traffic to achieve unified determination of parking confirmation, occupancy maintenance, and release boundaries. Experimental results show that the proposed method maintains good accuracy and robustness under complex conditions and can effectively support parking-occupancy detection in open-road scenarios.

Overall, this thesis focuses on roadside vehicle sensing under a single geomagnetic sensor setting and establishes a continuous technical chain from vehicle event extraction to vehicle type classification and parking-occupancy state determination. The results verify the feasibility of using single-node triaxial geomagnetic observations to support multi-task roadside vehicle sensing under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and easy deployment, and provide methodological reference for fine-grained smart-road governance and related system deployment.
