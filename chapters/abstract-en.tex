Roadside vehicle sensing for intelligent transportation requires continuous acquisition of key information, including vehicle passage, vehicle type, and parking and occupancy states, under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and long-term online operation. Compared with conventional sensing approaches such as video, radar, and inductive loops, a single geomagnetic sensor is compact, inexpensive, easy to embed in pavement, and less affected by illumination and occlusion, which makes it suitable for distributed long-term roadside monitoring. However, the information available from a single geomagnetic node is inherently limited. In addition, slow background magnetic drift, vehicle-speed variation, continuous traffic interference, and pseudo-stable states further increase the difficulty of upper-layer task recognition. To address these issues, this thesis takes continuous 50 Hz observations from a single roadside triaxial geomagnetic sensor as the research object, and studies three-class vehicle classification and parking-occupancy detection methods built on vehicle event extraction for single-geomagnetic roadside sensing. Vehicle event extraction is used to form a unified event-level input and to provide system support for the subsequent tasks. The main work of this thesis is as follows.

On the one hand, for the three-class vehicle classification problem under the single-geomagnetic setting, an event-segment-based classification dataset is constructed, and studies are carried out from two perspectives: lightweight edge-side deployment and offline enhanced analysis. The edge-side method extracts statistical quantities that reflect disturbance amplitude, energy, area, duration, and morphological characteristics, and establishes a lightweight classification baseline based on a Softmax classifier. To address waveform temporal stretching and local misalignment caused by vehicle-speed variation, the offline method introduces dynamic time warping and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network for enhanced classification. Experimental results show that the edge-side method can satisfy low-overhead deployment requirements, while the offline enhanced method can further improve classification performance and alleviate the confusion between medium-sized vehicles and adjacent classes.

On the other hand, for parking and occupancy detection in road scenarios with complex conditions such as slow environmental drift, continuous traffic flow, and pseudo-stable states, a dynamic-reference decision method based on signal stable points is proposed. This method extracts stable points through dual criteria over stable windows, constructs environmental reference stable points and occupancy reference stable points, and combines similarity gating, an outer state machine, and a degradation branch for continuous traffic to achieve unified determination of parking confirmation, occupancy maintenance, and release boundaries. Experimental results show that the proposed method maintains stable performance under normal conditions and exhibits good robustness under challenging conditions such as continuous traffic and slow drift.

Overall, this thesis investigates roadway vehicle sensing under the single-geomagnetic-sensor setting, and studies event-extraction-supported methods for three-class vehicle classification and roadside parking-occupancy detection. The results indicate that, under the constraints of low cost, low power consumption, and easy deployment, a single geomagnetic sensor has the potential to support roadside vehicle type recognition and parking-occupancy state sensing, and can provide methodological reference for related system deployment and function expansion.
