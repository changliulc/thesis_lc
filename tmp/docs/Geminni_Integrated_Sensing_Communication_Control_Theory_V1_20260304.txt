P001: [heading 1] Theoretical Foundations and Research Roadmap for Integrated Sensing, Communication, Computing, and Control (ISCCC) in Uncertainty-Resilient Autonomous Systems
P002: The trajectory of autonomous systems research has reached a critical juncture where the traditional silos of sensing, communication, computing, and control are no longer sufficient to guarantee safety in life-critical applications. The fundamental premise of modern autonomous driving, unmanned aerial systems, and industrial robotics is predicated on the assumption that individual subsystems can be optimized to a point of near-perfect reliability. However, empirical reality and theoretical constraints dictate that wireless communication links can never achieve absolute 100% reliability, nor can sensors provide 100% accurate representations of the physical world.1 In the presence of these inherent fallibilities, the current paradigm of sensor fusion—often reduced to a simple arbitration or voting mechanism between conflicting inputs—fails to provide the safety guarantees required for high-stakes environments. This report delineates a formal theory and a comprehensive research plan for the integration of sensing, communication, computing, and control (ISCCC), using the human driver’s intuitive "uncertainty resolution through control" as a foundational archetype.
P003: [heading 2] The Epistemological Crisis of Isolated Subsystems
P004: The primary challenge in safety-critical autonomous systems is the management of uncertainty. Current architectures typically treat sensing and communication as providers of "truth" to a control module. When these truths conflict, the system experiences an epistemological crisis. If a vehicle’s onboard LiDAR detects an obstacle but a roadside communication unit reports a clear path, the standard approach of sensor fusion seeks to resolve the conflict within the sensing or data-sharing domain.3 This resolution often takes the form of probabilistic arbitration, where the system "bets" on the most likely scenario based on historical sensor performance or signal-to-noise ratios.
P005: However, as each sensor is inherently fallible, the fused result is itself a probabilistic estimate that may, in certain edge cases, be less reliable than the output of a single, high-confidence sensor.4 For example, a LiDAR system might be blinded by a dust cloud, while a radar system sees through it but with much lower resolution. Late fusion techniques, which combine high-level object detections, might down-weight the LiDAR's "blip" as noise, even if it was a genuine detection of a high-reflectivity object.3 Conversely, early fusion preserved raw data but demands immense computational power and perfect spatial-temporal alignment, which is rarely achievable in dynamic, high-speed scenarios.3
P006: [heading 3] The Limits of Communication and Sensing Reliability
P007: Wireless communication, particularly in the 6G era, is increasingly viewed not just as a pipe for data but as a sensor in its own right—a concept known as Integrated Sensing and Communication (ISAC).1 Yet, even with these advances, the physical layer remains constrained by channel attenuation, dynamic interference, and the crowded nature of the electromagnetic spectrum.6 In industrial assembly workshops or low-altitude wireless networks (LAWN), these constraints manifest as high closed-loop latency and synchronization errors that degrade the performance of multi-agent formation control.7
P008: The core of the problem lies in the fact that neither a single sensor nor a fused suite of sensors can eliminate the residual "epistemic uncertainty"—the uncertainty stemming from a lack of knowledge or hidden states.2 When autonomous systems rely on fused information that is only 99.9% accurate, they are essentially gambling with the remaining 0.1%, which is unacceptable in life-critical scenarios.
P009: [heading 2] Formalizing the Human "Slowing Down" Strategy
P010: The narrative of the experienced human driver offers a transformative solution. When presented with a conflict—his eyes see no obstacle, but a roadside sign warns of one—he does not perform an immediate "arbitration" to decide who is right. Instead, he modifies his control state by slowing down. This simple control action serves multiple integrated purposes:
P011: Safety Margin Expansion: By reducing velocity, the driver increases the available "braking distance" and "reaction time," effectively moving the system into a safer region of the state-space.
P012: Information Density Increase: At a lower speed, the sensing system (eyes) and communication system (monitoring other drivers) can gather more samples over the same spatial distance, increasing the signal-to-noise ratio of the perception task.
P013: Active Probing: The driver’s behavior acts as a probing signal. If other nearby drivers also slow down, it communicates a collective confirmation of the obstacle's presence via their observable behaviors.
P014: Sequential Hypothesis Testing: The driver treats the situation as an ongoing experiment, continuing the "sensing-communication-computing" loop until the evidence for one hypothesis (obstacle vs. no obstacle) reaches a predefined confidence threshold.
P015: This behavior demonstrates that the problem of conflicting sensing or communication is not a sensing problem or a communication problem; it is a control-mediated information-seeking problem. In the ISCCC framework, control is not just the end of the pipeline; it is a proactive participant in the acquisition and verification of information.9
P016: [heading 2] Mathematical Foundations of the ISCCC Framework
P017: To generalise the human driver’s behavior into a formal theory, we must unify several mathematical domains: Dual Control Theory, Information Theory, and Risk-Aware Control Barrier Functions.
P018: [heading 3] Dual Control Theory and Active Sensing
P019: The driver’s strategy is a physical implementation of Dual Control Theory. In this framework, control signals have two roles: "cautionary control" (maintaining the state near the target) and "probing control" (exciting the system to learn more about the unknown parameters).11 For a linear uncertain system, the control input  is designed not just to minimize a tracking error , but also to minimize an information-theoretic cost .

P021: [heading 3] Risk-Aware Control Barrier Functions (CBF) and CVaR
P022: The "safety margin" in the driver story is formalized through the use of Control Barrier Functions (CBF). A CBF defines a "safe set" in the state space and provides a mathematical guarantee that the system will remain within this set if certain inequalities are satisfied.8 However, traditional CBFs assume the obstacle's position is known perfectly. In the ISCCC framework, we use Risk-Aware CBFs that incorporate the Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR).8
P023: CVaR measures the expected loss in the "tail" of the probability distribution (the worst-case scenarios). When the sensing uncertainty is high—represented by a wider variance in the obstacle’s predicted position—the CVaR increases. The ISCCC controller responds by "tightening" the barrier constraint, which forces the vehicle to slow down or steer further away, effectively expanding the safety margin in real-time.8
P024: [heading 3] Value of Information (VoI) vs. Age of Information (AoI)
P025: The timing of information is as critical as its content. The Age of Information (AoI) measures the freshness of data—how long it has been since the last update was received.19 While AoI is useful for scheduling transmissions, it does not capture the utility of the data for the control task. The ISCCC framework prioritizes the Value of Information (VoI), which quantifies the reduction in uncertainty (estimation error) at the controller enabled by a new update.19
P026: For the human driver, the roadside sign has high VoI because it introduces a significant change in the "belief" about the road ahead. If the system matrix  of the environment is unstable (e.g., a fast-moving pedestrian), the uncertainty grows non-linearly over time, making the VoI of a fresh update exponentially more valuable than in a stable environment.19
P027: [heading 2] The Integrated Sensing, Communication, Computing, and Control (ISCCC) Architecture
P028: The realization of the ISCCC framework requires a deep coupling across the 6G stack. This is often described as a "reflex arc" architecture, where sensing, communication, and computing act as the sensory-neural-cerebral pathway for the control "muscles".1
P029: [heading 3] The Reflex Arc in 6G UAV Swarms
P030: In UAV swarms, the ISCCC framework is implemented through a three-layer structure 1:
P031: Flying Layer: Terrestrial base stations (BS) provide ISCCC links to maintain basic flight stability and trajectory accuracy through real-time state estimation.1
P032: Self-Organizing Layer: Inter-swarm ISCCC links enable the swarm to act as a virtual movable antenna system, coordinating their physical positions to optimize communication and sensing coverage simultaneously.1
P033: Functioning Layer: Swarm-to-terminal links handle task-specific requirements like mobile edge computing (MEC) and secure data sharing.1
P034: Through a case study of UAV trajectory error minimization, it has been shown that jointly designing the four components using the ISCCC principle can simultaneously improve reliability and efficiency compared to systems using Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) alone.1 The ISCCC-enabled system can realize real-time dynamic control command updates based on "on-the-fly" sensing, allowing the swarm to adapt to wind disturbances or signal occlusions that would otherwise lead to mission failure.1
P035: [heading 3] Integrated Sensing, Communication, and Computation (ISCC) in Edge AI
P036: The computing component of ISCCC is vital for Edge AI tasks. In systems like Over-the-Air Federated Edge Learning (Air-FEEL), sensing (for data acquisition), communication (for sharing model gradients), and computation (for local updates) are tightly coupled.23 These processes compete for the same network resources—time, bandwidth, and energy.23
P037: A formal ISCC algorithm handles this by alternately optimizing the batch size of sensed data and the allocation of network resources. It has been found that for each device, the optimal computation speed is the minimum one that satisfies the latency constraint, allowing more energy to be diverted to sensing and communication to improve the learning convergence rate.23 This demonstrates that the "computing" in ISCCC is not just about raw power, but about the timing and efficiency of processing in a way that supports the sensing and control loop.
P038: [heading 3] Semantic Communication for Control Updates
P039: To further optimize the communication-control link, the ISCCC framework utilizes Semantic Communication (SC). Instead of transmitting every raw measurement, an AI-empowered Semantic Feature Extractor (SFE) at the transmitter infers the "meaning" of the data relative to the control task.25 If the current state of the vehicle is highly correlated with previous states (e.g., traveling on a straight highway), the system may choose not to transmit an update, relying instead on a Semantic Feature Reconstructor (SFR) at the receiver to predict the control information.25
P040: This predictive approach reduces communication overhead while maintaining control performance, allowing the system to handle the "non-Markovian" nature of complex tasks where current decisions are influenced by a long history of past states.25 This mimics the driver's ability to maintain a mental model of the road, only updating it when "surprising" new information (like the roadside sign) is encountered.
P041: [heading 2] Generalizing the Theory to Wider Domains
P042: While autonomous driving is the most intuitive example, the ISCCC formal theory is applicable across all domains where safety and efficiency are constrained by uncertainty.
P043: [heading 3] Industrial Automation and AGV Control
P044: In automobile assembly workshops, multiple Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) must coordinate in a dynamic environment with human workers and shifting obstacles.7 Traditional decentralized designs lead to high communication overhead and synchronization errors. An ISCCC-based closed-loop scheme allows AGVs to switch between "active local decision-making" (based on high-confidence local sensing) and "passive reception of BS decision-making" (based on roadside info).7
P045: By using a Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance Risk Map quantified by CVaR, the AGVs can reserve sufficient time for smooth obstacle avoidance, reducing the risk of "emergency braking" that causes material damage or bottlenecks.7 Simulation results indicate such an integrated approach can improve production capacity by over 62% compared to conventional isolated schemes.7
P046: [heading 3] Human-Robot Collaboration and Intention Recognition
P047: In the domain of collaborative robots (Cobots), "sensing" is not just about physical obstacles but about human intentions.26 The ISCCC framework treats a human partner as a "sub-optimal reinforcement learning agent" whose intentions must be inferred from multimodal behavioral data (video, audio, haptic).26
P048: When the robot senses conflicting signals regarding human intent, it applies the "slowing down" principle through Neural Admittance Control.26 By adjusting its "admittance" (resistance to motion), the robot minimizes interaction forces and fluctuations, creating a "safety shield" while it gathers more data to synchronize its actions with the human partner.26 This transition from "optimal efficiency" to "intention-aligned safety" is a hallmark of the ISCCC theory.

P052: The information gain of a sensing process is a non-linear function of the system’s control state. Control actions can be intentionally designed to minimize the conditional entropy of the environment's state distribution, even at the temporary sacrifice of task-specific progress.

P058: [heading 2] Research Plan for Generalization and Formalization
P059: To advance the ISCCC paradigm from its current nascent stage to a rigorous, industry-standard theory, a multi-phase research plan is formulated.
P060: [heading 3] Phase 1: Mathematical Unification and Dual-Control Expansion (Years 1-2)
P061: The objective is to refine the mathematical links between Information Theory and Control Theory.
P062: Belief-Space Planning Algorithms: Develop new Model Predictive Control (MPC) frameworks that operate directly on the belief-space (probability distributions) rather than state-space.8
P063: Sequential Hypothesis Testing Integration: Formulate non-asymptotic lower bounds for "stopping times" in sequential tests conducted over ergodic Markov chains, allowing the system to know exactly when it has gathered enough information to accelerate back to normal speed.18
P064: CVaR-CBF Generalization: Extend risk-aware control barrier functions to handle non-Gaussian, multi-modal uncertainty distributions using Wasserstein Barycenters.8
P065: [heading 3] Phase 2: Hardware-in-the-Loop and Signal-Level Design (Years 2-3)
P066: The objective is to design the "ISCCC Reflex Arc" at the signal and hardware level.
P067: Control-Aware Waveform Engineering: Design 6G waveforms that are optimized for simultaneous data transmission, high-resolution radar sensing, and "over-the-air computation" (AirComp) to minimize computing latency.23
P068: STAR-RIS for ISCCC: Investigate the use of Simultaneously Transmitting and Reflecting Reconfigurable Intelligent Surfaces (STAR-RIS) to provide full-space coverage for sensing and communication in complex urban "canyons" where signal blockage is common.28
P069: Semantic Compression Engines: Develop hardware-accelerated Semantic Feature Extractors (SFE) that can identify "control-critical" information in less than 10ms.25
P070: [heading 3] Phase 3: Domain-Specific Application and Scalability (Years 3-4)
P071: The objective is to validate the theory in diverse, complex scenarios.
P072: UAV Swarm Self-Organization: Implement ISCCC in high-speed UAV formation flying, where the "probing signals" of individual agents are used to detect atmospheric disturbances (wind shear) that sensors cannot directly see.1
P073: Surgical Robotics Case Study: Apply ISCCC to remote surgery, where communication latency and sensing uncertainty (tissue deformation) are life-critical. The "slowing down" principle here translates to the robot pausing or reducing force until haptic feedback and visual data are synchronized.29
P074: Human-in-the-Loop Driving Benchmarks: Conduct user studies to establish "human-like" behavior benchmarks for AVs, ensuring that the "slowing down" behavior is assertive enough for safety but not so over-cautious that it surprises other road users.30
P075: [heading 3] Phase 4: Standardization and Certification Frameworks (Years 5+)
P076: The objective is to create the regulatory and technical standards for ISCCC-enabled systems.
P077: Formal Verification for SHS: Develop automated verification tools for Stochastic Hybrid Systems (SHS) that can provide "formal probabilistic certificates" of system safety.32
P078: ISCCC Metrics Standardization: Establish industry-standard metrics for the "Value of Information" and "Risk-Aware Safety Margins" to replace the simplistic "percentage reliability" currently used in safety certification.
P079: [heading 2] The Future Outlook of ISCCC
P080: The integration of sensing, communication, computing, and control is not merely a technical upgrade; it is a fundamental shift in how we approach the "intelligence" of machines. By adopting a "reflex arc" architecture and formalizing the human-like ability to trade speed for certainty, we can create systems that are robust to the inevitable failures of individual components.
P081: The future of autonomous driving, agile UAV swarms, and collaborative industrial robots lies in their ability to navigate the "gray zones" of conflicting information. The ISCCC formal theory provides the roadmap for this journey, moving from a world of "blind arbitration" to one of "informed, control-mediated resolution." As 6G networks mature, the ability to orchestrate these four pillars as an inseparable, feedback-driven entity will be the defining characteristic of the next generation of cyber-physical systems.10 Through this integration, the "slowing down" of a vehicle today will become the foundation for the "moving forward" of global autonomous infrastructure tomorrow.
