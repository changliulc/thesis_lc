ABSTRACT
Roadside sensing technology provides real-time access to traffic operating states from the
global perspective of infrastructure and serves as a key support for traffic organization optimization, refined parking management, and intelligent transportation systems. As urban
traffic becomes more complex and traffic governance becomes increasingly refined, there
is a growing demand for low-cost, highly reliable roadside vehicle sensing data that can
support long-term online operation. Vehicle composition information, together with parking and road occupancy states, forms an important basis for traffic performance evaluation,
parking management, and road-space governance. Compared with video- and radar-based
approaches, geomagnetic sensors offer advantages such as low cost, ease of deployment, and
all-weather operation, making them suitable for long-term roadside monitoring. However,
the information provided by a single geomagnetic node is limited and is susceptible to slow
environmental magnetic drift, vehicle-speed variation, continuous traffic disturbances, and
pseudo-stable states, all of which make feature extraction and state recognition challenging. To address these issues, this thesis investigates vehicle classification and parking-andoccupancy-state determination using continuous 50 Hz observations from a roadside singlepoint triaxial geomagnetic sensor, with unified event-level inputs as the basis. The main
contributions are summarized as follows.
First, to address the difficulty of vehicle recognition caused by vehicle-speed variation and
waveform distortion, a three-class vehicle classification method that balances lightweight
edge deployment and high offline accuracy is proposed. A geomagnetic vehicle-event dataset
collected from real traffic scenes is first constructed. For edge-side deployment, multidimensional statistical features reflecting magnetic disturbance amplitude, energy, temporal area,
and morphological distribution are extracted and fused to build a low-overhead Softmaxbased baseline classifier. For offline enhancement, dynamic time warping is introduced to
align temporal features under speed-induced waveform stretching and local misalignment,
and a one-dimensional convolutional neural network is designed for deep feature extraction
and enhanced classification. Experimental results show that this classification framework
satisfies the computational constraints of edge devices while effectively improving class separability and mitigating confusion between adjacent vehicle categories.
