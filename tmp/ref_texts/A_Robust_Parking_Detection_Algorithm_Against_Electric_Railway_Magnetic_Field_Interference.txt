A Robust Parking Detection Algorithm Against
Electric Railway Magnetic Field Interference
Zusheng Zhang , Xianmang He, and Huaqiang Y uan
Abstract— Wireless magnetic sensor networks (WMSNs) offer
an effective and efﬁcient low-cost alternative for real-time park-
ing space monitoring. Current algorithms used for parking space
detection based on the WMSNs have been well studied but not
without eliminating the geomagnetic ﬁeld interference. Electric
railway systems, such as subways and light rail systems, produce
magnetic ﬁeld radiation that interferes with the geomagnetic ﬁeld.
The electric railway magnetic ﬁeld interference has increased and
is prominent in urban settings; therefore, it is imperative to ﬁlter
out the interference in order to increase the detection accuracy.
This paper proposes an algorithm for parking space detection,
which can effectively eliminate the geomagnetic background
interference. We solve the parking detection problem through
signal ﬁltering technology. Based on mathematical morphology,
we designed two ﬁlters for extracting morphological structures
from interfered magnetic signals. The experimental results verify
that the proposed algorithm is mo re accurate than the existing
algorithms.
Index Terms— Vehicle detection, magnetic sensor, interference,
morphological ﬁlter.
I. I NTRODUCTION
C
URRENTLY, real-time parking demand and availability
has become a major issue in densely populated urban
settings since parking spaces are limited and the number
of vehicles is continually in creasing [1]. Searching for an
available parking space during peak hours is usually difﬁcult
and leads to additional energy waste, trafﬁc congestion and air
pollution. The need for real time parking monitoring systems
is signiﬁcant and continues to be in demand.
Parking detection mechanisms can be categorized into two
types: indoor and outdoor. There are various methods for
indoor parking detection, for example: video cameras [2],
ultrasound [3], and infrared radiation [4]. However, this type
of technology is not viable outdoors because installing cables
and wires needed for energy supply and data transmission is
not cost effective. Thus, a total w ireless solution is a feasible
option. Wireless Magnetic Sensor Networks (WMSNs) have
low power consumption, are small and cost-effective [5].

Also, they are inherently immune to environmental factors
such as rain, wind, snow or fog than traditional wired tech-
nologies. Recently an outdoor parking detection WMSN, for
about 5,000 parking spaces, is being gradually implemented
in Shenzhen, China.
In this parking monitoring system, magnetic sensor nodes
are placed at the center of each parking space and the sensor
periodically samples the geomagnetic ﬁeld. When a vehicle
enters or leaves the parking space, the parking detection
algorithm can detect the change in the parking space status and
generates detection events. Detection events data is then col-
lected and transmitted to a server. Having collected the event
data from these synchronized sensor nodes, the server can
calculate the parking duration of each vehicle. This data can be
used for different types of applications, such as parking nav-
igation, parking collection fees, illegal parking detection, etc.
The geomagnetic ﬁeld is highly susceptible to interference.
The operation of electric railway systems, such as subway
and light rail systems, produces a wide range of magnetic
ﬁeld radiation, which interferes with the geomagnetic ﬁeld.
Santarelli et al. [6] analyzed the geomagnetic background
noise at L ’ Aquila geomagnetic observatory during 2006
and 2007, and they found that the main source of artiﬁcial
noise is constituted by the Direct Current (DC) electriﬁed
railways. Lowes [7] found that DC electric railways pro-
duce magnetic ﬁelds mainly from unintended earth-leakage
currents. Pirjola [8] proposed a computational model for the
magnetic ﬁeld produced by DC railways assuming that the
leakage current density changes linearly. Currently, many cities
are building subway systems, so the magnetic interference has
become a factor to consider when deploying a WMSN.
There are numerous research studies of parking detec-
tion algorithms based on wireless magnetic sensor networks.
However, these algorithms presume that the background geo-
magnetic ﬁeld is without interference. They do not include
mechanisms to counter the interference and are prone to pro-
duce high false and missed detec tion rates in environments that
have magnetic interference. We propose a parking detection
algorithm which can effectively eliminate the geomagnetic
ﬁeld interference. Our main contributions are as follows:
(1) This paper introduces the problems of magnetic ﬁeld
interference caused by electric railway and proposes an
“eliminating interference” algorithm for parking detection.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the ﬁrst work to study the
geomagnetic interference problem for a WMSN based parking
monitoring system.

II. R ELA TED WORKS
A considerable amount of research has been conducted on
the use of WMSN for trafﬁc information collection. Cheung
and V araiya [9] explored the applications for vehicle detection,
speed estimation, and classiﬁcation. Wang et al. [10], [11]
proposed a similarity based vehicle detection algorithm to
detect vehicles in low SNR (Signal-to-noise ratio) condi-
tions by calculating the correlation [12] between sampled
signals and a reference signal. Kwong et al. [13] presents
a scheme to estimate the travel time distribution of a sin-
gle arterial link, spanning several signalized intersections.
Taghvaeeyan and Rajamani [14] presents a portable roadside
magnetic sensor system for vehicle counting, classiﬁcation,
and speed measurement.
Parking detection based on WMSN has been present in both
the research community and industry (e.g., ParkSol). Thresh-
old and state machine mechanisms are used to trace the energy
change of the magnetic ﬁeld when a vehicle is parked [15].
If the magnetic ﬁeld exceeds a threshold, the sensor will
indicate the space is occupi ed. Cheung and V araiya [9]
proposed an adaptive threshold algorithm can be used for
parking detection. Ding and Cheung [16] proposed a min-
max detection algorithm. Modiﬁed min-max algorithm for
magnetometers [17] has also been published. Zhu and Y u [18]
proposed a parking detection algorithm that combines state
machine and cross correlation mechanisms. Zhang et al. [19]
proposed an algorithm for parking occupancy detection based
on the distance discriminate analysis method.
Some works have studied the problem of adjacent inter-
ference. Trigona et al. [20] conducted the magnetic inter-
ference test of the adjacent vehicle in bay parking spaces.
Balid et al. [21] found that the magneto-resistive sensor
node is easily disturbed by the vehicles which have high-
intensity magnetic ﬁeld (such as large-scale trucks, large
buses, etc.) on nearby lanes, causing false detection problems
and they found that the Z-axis was less affected by interfer-
ence. Liu and Zheng [22] analy zed the interference problem
caused by the neighboring vehicles and studied the inﬂuence
of parking direction and sensor coordinate direction on the
magnetic ﬁeld strength.
Some researchers propose to add other types of sensors
for auxiliary detection. Sifuentes et al. [23] have proposed
a wireless sensor node based on two sensors (one magnetic
and one optical) to detect the presence of a vehicle. When a
vehicle arrives or departs, an op tical sensor detects the change
in light and wakes up the magnetic sensor which measures the
magnetic ﬁeld in order to detect the vehicle. Huang et al. [24]
also designed a similar node to solve the adjacent interference
problem.
In summary, the vehicle detection for WMSN in the non-
interference environment has been studied extensively. Some
works have discussed the adjacent i nterference. However, there
is no relevant work to study the issue of geomagnetic ﬁeld
interference caused by an electric railway.

The main task of the algorithm is to accurately extract two
types of structural signals from the interfered signal: Gaussian-
like pulse and rectangular structure. We adopt a mathematical
morphology theory [29] to design signal ﬁlters which can
effectively ﬁlter noise and extract morphological structures.
The advantage of mathematical morphological ﬁltering is that
the algorithm is simple and requires less computation. It does
not need to consider the type of noise and only needs to select
structural elements that match the target signal. This method
is suitable for sensor nodes to process signals in real time.

Using the structural elemen ts of the same type and size,

Open-closing operation:

result of open-closing operation has the lower amplitude than
the original signal and the result of close-opening operation
has the larger amplitude than the original signal. This may
affect the result of rectangle structure detection. So a mean
weighted combination method is adopted as equation (13).

VI. C ONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION
Wireless magnetic sensor networks are being gradually used
for outdoor parking detection. However, the geomagnetic ﬁeld
is highly susceptible to interference from electric railway
systems. This work proposes a parking detection algorithm
which can ﬁlter and eliminate the geomagnetic interference.
Two ﬁlters based on mathematical morphology are proposed
to remove the magnetic interference and extract the structural
signals of vehicle parking detection. Experiment results show
that the detection accuracy of the algorithm is more accurate
than previous algorithms for parking detection.
For the future study, we plan to measure another magnitude
which is not affected by magnetic interference to improve the
detection accuracy.
(1) We can add a second sensor for auxiliary detection,
for example, a Light Dependent Resistor (LDR) to measure
optical changes of light, or sensors such as infrared, ultra-
sonic, Doppler radar, and so on. However, these sensors are
often susceptible in outdoor environment, such as weather
conditions, occlusions, and lighting variations. Conversely,
if a second sensor is used, the design of the node’s shell needs
to be changed. For example, if an optical sensor is added,
the diffusion of light should be considered in the design of the
shell. We need to conduct further research on the feasibility
and cost effectiveness of adding a second sensor.
(2) The change of RSSI (Received Signal Strength Indica-
tion) of the node’s wireless communication can also be used as
a reference for detection. In th e process of a vehicle entering
and leaving the parking space, if the sensor node tries to send
and receive data packets multip le times, its parent node and
itself will both sense that the RSSI has changed signiﬁcantly.
However, passing vehicles, pedestrians, and adjacent vehicles’
parking will also change the value of RSSI. Therefore, it is
necessary to further explore the relationship between the
change of RSSI and parking detection.
