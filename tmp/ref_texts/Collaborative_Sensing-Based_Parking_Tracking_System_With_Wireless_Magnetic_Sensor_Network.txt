Collaborative Sensing-Based Parking T racking
System With Wireless Magnetic Sensor Network
Zusheng Zhang , Xufei Mao, Kunxiao Zhou, and Huaqiang Y uan
Abstract —Wireless magnetic sensor networks provide a
low-cost technology for the outdoor real-time parking tracking
system. However, the magnetic sensor is susceptible to the
interference caused by vehicles parked in adjacent spaces.
Most existing detection systems are solely based on local
sampled data from the sensor node and they are prone to
high false and missed detection rates in the case of a low
signal-to-noise ratio. This paper proposes a collaborative
sensing parking tracking system by fusing the signals of
the adjacent sensors to eliminate the interference. Sensor
nodes convert magnetic signals into signatures represented
by peak values, and then the base station computes the spatial
relationships between adjacent signatures to identify parking
event or interference. An experiment system includes sixty sensor nodes that were deployed in bay parking spaces.
Experiment results veriﬁed that the proposed system has better detection accuracy than existing systems.
Index Terms— Magnetic sensor, parking tracking system, collaborative sensing, wireless sensor network.
I. I NTRODUCTION
P
ROBLEMS arising from the gap between the demand
and supply of parking spaces ar e becoming increasingly
acute in most urban environment. An automated parking track-
ing system is an intelligent transportation system technology
where the presence of parked vehicles is monitored in real-
time. This technology can be used by drivers to ﬁnd parking
efﬁciently while minimizing time and effort.
Parking space tracking systems are categorized into two
types: indoor and outdoor. Technologies currently employed
indoors, such as ultrasonic [1], image (camera) [2], are not
suitable for large scale appli cations in outdoor because they
are susceptible to outdoor environmental interference, such as
weather conditions, occlusions, and lighting variations. Based
on the fact that most vehicles have ferrous metals, a Wireless
Magnetic Sensor Network (WMSN) [3]–[5] is a viable and
pragmatic alternative for an outdoor parking tracking system.

WMSN is wireless communication system, battery powered,
and its life expectancy is more th an 5 years. Recently, wireless
magnetic sensor networks are gradually used for outdoor
parking detection.
In a WMSN parking tracking system, the sensor node is
deployed at the center of each parking space. The sensor
samples the geomagnetic ﬁeld periodically. When a vehicle
enters or leaves the space, the sensor detects the status change
and generates parking event. However, a vehicle’s magnetic
ﬁeld is not only the target of detection but also the source
of interference. Adjacent inte rference is created by vehicles
parked on adjacent spaces. There are three types of parking
spaces: bay, parallel and angled. In the case of bay parking,
the distance between the sensor node and adjacent parked
vehicle is shorter than angled and parallel parking. Therefore,
adjacent interference is signi ﬁcant and more prominent in bay
parking. All of the following discussion of this work is based
on the bay parking spaces.
Most of the existing WMSN based parking tracking systems
make an occupied or vacant decision solely on the local sam-
pled data of the sensor. The sensors in bay parking spaces are
susceptible to the adjacent interference and result in high false
and missed detection rates. Zhu et al. [6] proposed a hybrid
detection algorithm based on local threshold detection and
data fusion. However, in the actual parking tracking system,
these thresholds of local detection are difﬁcult to accurately
estimate. This paper proposes a collaborative sensing based
parking tracking system. Our main contributions are given as
follows:

II. R ELA TED WORKS
Parking tracking system w ith WMSN has been present in
both the research community and industry. Threshold detection
algorithms [7] usually are used to measure the geomagnetic
ﬁeld change after the vehicle is parked, if the change contin-
uous exceeds a threshold, the sensor will indicate the space
is occupied. Cheung et al. [8] proposed an adaptive thresh-
old algorithm for parking detection. Ding and Cheung [9]
proposed a min-max detection algorithm. Modiﬁed min-max
algorithm for magnetometers [10] has also been published.
Zhu et al. [11] proposed a parking detection algorithm by
calculating the correlation bet ween the sampled signals and a
referential signal. Zhang and Y u [12] proposed an algorithm
for parking occupancy detection based on the distance dis-
criminate analysis method.
Some works have studied the problem of adjacent inter-
ference. Trigona et al. [13] conducted the magnetic interfer-
ence test of the adjacent vehic le in the bay parking spaces.
Saiﬁl et al. [14] found that the Z-axis was less affected by
interference. Liu and Zheng [15] analyzed the interference
problem caused by the neighboring vehicles and studied the
inﬂuence of parking direction and sensor coordinate direction
on the magnetic ﬁeld strength. These methods choose the
signal with the least interference (e.g. Z-axis’ signal) for vehi-
cle detection. They ignore the sp atial correlation of adjacent
signals and are not reliable.
Zhu et al. [6] proposed a parking detection method includes
two parts: 1) Local detection algorithm, each node uses hard
thresholds and state machine m echanism for parking detection;
2) Data fusion based on Dempste r-Shafer evidence, when the
local detection algorithm cannot achieve accurate detection,
they use the Dempster-Shafer evidence theory for the ﬁnal
detection. However, their al gorithm is mainly based on local
data detection, and more than 90% of the parking detection
only depends on local data for decision-making. The hard
thresholds for local detection are related to many factors, such
as the actual deployment environment, the model of the sensor,
the gain settings of sensor (e.g., there are eight settings for
the gain of HMC5983), and so on. The disadvantage of this
method is that its hard thresholds are difﬁcult to estimate
accurately.
Some researchers propose to add other types of sensors
for auxiliary detection. Farhad et al. [16] employed four
sensors, magnetic, light, temperature, and ambient temperature
sensors of the motes for reliable detection of the free space.
Sifuentes et al. [17] designed a node based on two sensors (one
magnetic and one optical) to detect the presence of a vehicle.
When a vehicle arrives or departs, an optical sensor detects
the change of light and wakes up the magnetic sensor which
measures the magnetic ﬁeld to detect the vehicle. However,
these sensors are often susceptible to the outdoor physical
interference such as a dust blocked optical sensor which can’t
work effectively.

B. Interference of Moving Vehicle
The moving vehicle in the adj acent road produces the mag-
netic interference signal, and the interference can be discuss
as following two cases:
(1) While the status of a sensor node is ‘vacant’, some
vehicles which have high-intensity magnetic ﬁeld passed in the
adjacent lane. As shown in Fig. 10 , the magnetic interference
signals, generated by moving vehicles, are pulse signals. The
characteristic of a stable change is a necessary condition for
‘occupied’ event detection, so that pulse signals produced by
moving vehicles will not affect the ‘occupied’ event detection.
(2) While the status of a sensor node is ‘occupied’, there
has interference caused by moving vehicle in the adjacent
road. As shown in Fig. 11 , the interference signal is a
pulse signal. The criteria for ‘vacant’ vent detection is as
follow: if a vehicle left, there must has Change (Entering )+
Change (Leaving ) ≈ 0, take Fig. 11 for example,
CE + CL ≈ 0. So pulse signals produced by moving vehicles
do not lead to false detection of ‘vacant’ event.

VI. C ONCLUSION
Recently, WMSN is being gradually used as an outdoor
parking tracking system. However, the magnetic signal is
susceptible to the interferenc e by vehicles parked in adjacent
spaces. This paper proposes a collaborative sensing based
tracking system to eliminate adjacent interference. Sensor
nodes convert magnetic signals into signatures represented by
peak values, and BS computes the pattern similarity between
adjacent signatures to identify p arking event and interference.
Experiment results show that the system has better detection
accuracy than existing systems.
