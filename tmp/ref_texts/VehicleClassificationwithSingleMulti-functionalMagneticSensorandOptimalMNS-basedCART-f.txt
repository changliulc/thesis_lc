abstract
To take advantages of magnetic sensor technology in terms of cost, size, weight, power
consumption and wireless communication, a wireless multi-functional magnetic sensor
was designed and developed. Then, a novel method with single multi-functional magnetic
sensor and optimal Minimum Number of Split-sample (MNS)-based Classiﬁcation and
Regression Tree (CART) algorithm was proposed in this paper to classify on-road vehicles.
The sensor was deployed on the road to acquire real-time vehicle waveform data. The
decision tree model based on CART algorithm was used to execute on-line vehicle classi-
ﬁcation in the sensor node. Eight speed-independent time-domain waveform features
were extracted as the model inputs. This paper trained the decision tree model by using
vehicle samples derived from the multi-functional magnetic sensor and pruned the opti-
mal decision tree with a Minimum Error Pruning (MEP) rule to obtain an optimal pruning
tree which is more robust to new samples. Some experiments were implemented by dif-
ferent sample sets and classiﬁcation methods. The results showed that the proposed
method achieved on-line vehicle classiﬁcation in the sensor node. For the ﬁeld sample
sets with two vehicle classes, the average accuracy rates of test samples were 88.9%
and 94.4% in the original samples and swapping samples respectively. Besides higher
accuracy, the method also has a better sample robustness, which is easy to classify
new samples. The comparison results of current methods also showed that the proposed
method has some advantages in aspects of accuracy rate, sample robustness and execu-
tion time.

1. Introduction
In recent years, Intelligent Transportation System (ITS)
programs have been initiated in many countries [1].A sa
part of ITS, on-line vehicle classiﬁcation system plays an
important role since it gathers some critical parts of the
data used in ITS.
Over the past few years, vehicle classiﬁcation has
been widely studied and become an important source of
information for transportation design and management.
A vehicle classiﬁcation system is essential for trafﬁc
management, automatic toll collection, parking optimiza-
tion, law enforcement, autonomous navigation, etc. [2].
Moreover, transportation agencies collect vehicle classiﬁ-
cation information to create highway maintenance plan,
evaluate highway usage and optimize the deployment of
various resources on the road [3]. Electronic toll collec-
tion and automatic enforcement also require accurate
identiﬁcation of vehicle classes on highways so that
vehicles of different classes are charged accordingly
[4,5]. In trafﬁc safety research and implementation, the

analyze crash data with certain vehicle classes, which
support the derivation of more effective countermeasure

Hence, these needs have made engineers and researchers

Currently, various technologies are used for vehicle
classiﬁcation. Loop [7,8], video [2,9,10], acoustic [11,12],
and other sensors [13] are used in vehicle classiﬁcation
systems, and a large number of researchers devote them-
selves to the models and algorithms for vehicle classiﬁca-
tion by those sensors. Jeng et al. [8] proposed a two-step
algorithm to execute vehicle classiﬁcation using the
inductive loop signature technology. Employing some
speed estimation advances, Coifman and Kim [7] brought
length based vehicle classiﬁcation to single-loop detec-
tors. Then their classiﬁcation methodology was evaluated
against concurrent measurements from video and dual-
loop detectors. For video and acoustic sensors, Kafai and
Bhanu [2] presented a stochastic multiclass vehicle classi-
ﬁcation system which classiﬁes a vehicle into one of four
classes: sedan, pickup truck, SUV/minivan, and unknown.
A feature set of tail light and vehicle dimensions was
extracted which fed a feature selection algorithm to
deﬁne a low-dimensional feature vector. To obtain more
complete knowledge regarding vehicles’ acoustic charac-
teristics, Guo et al. [11] proposed a fusion approach to
combine two sets of features, in which various aspects
of an acoustic signature were emphasized individually.
Next, they proposed a modiﬁed Bayesian based fusion
method to take advantage of matching each speciﬁc fea-
ture set with its favored classiﬁer. Thus, feature-based
methods have been commonly used for vehicle
classiﬁcation.
However, existing technologies such as piezoelectric
sensors, inductive loops and video imaging have very
high installation and maintenance costs. Some technolo-
gies such as infrared sensors, video imaging are sensitive
to trafﬁc, environmental, and weather conditions [3].
Furthermore, the performance of microwave radar,
infrared sensor and video imaging is often affected by
Doppler effects or noise introduced by moving vehicles
and terrain variations. In contrast, magnetic sensors are
less sensitive to weather, terrain, noise and Doppler
effects. Moreover, magnetic sensors have advantages in
terms of cost, size, weight, power consumption and
wireless communication [14]. Hence, applying magnetic
sensors to vehicle classiﬁcation is a promising research
topic.
Recently, some methods on vehicle classiﬁcation or re-
identiﬁcation utilizing magnetic sensors have been
reported in literature [1,3,5,6,15]. In order to improve the
recognition rate by a single magnetic sensor, Lan et al.
[1] proposed a novel feature extraction algorithm and a
novel vehicle classiﬁcation and recognition algorithm.
Based on the features of the concavity and convexity areas,
and the angles of concave and convex parts of the wave-
form, they developed an improved support vector machine
(ISVM) classiﬁer to perform vehicle classiﬁcation and rec-
ognition. A wireless sensor network (WSN) for vehicle clas-
siﬁcation based on axle count and spacing was designed,
calibrated, tested, and deployed near a weigh station in
Sunol, California [3]. Bajwa et al. collected 53 different
trucks exiting the weigh station and applied the Axle
Detection algorithm (ADET) to successfully classify all 53
trucks. Kaewkamnerd et al. [5] studied vehicle classiﬁca-
tion system based the features of vehicle magnetic length,
averaged energy, and Hill-pattern peaks. They classiﬁed
vehicles into 4 types: motorcycle, car, pickup and van.
Their system automatically detected vehicles, extracted
features, and classiﬁed them with an average accuracy rate
about 79.66%. Cheung and Varaiya [6] studied vehicle clas-
siﬁcation using magnetic sensors based on the 13-class
Federal Highway Administration (FHWA) scheme. They
studied three classiﬁers (k-Nearest Neighbor, Support Vec-
tor Machine and Direct Hill-Pattern Matching) in different
datasets. Their average accuracy rate was about 70%. If the
feature of magnetic length was considered, the average
accuracy rate will be slightly improved. However, in order
to obtain accurate magnetic length, more magnetic sensors
are needed. The existing methods are either developed
with too small sample size to verify the statistical robust-
ness or lack of execution time analysis which cannot reveal
the effectiveness for on-line vehicle classiﬁcation in mag-
netic sensor nodes. Besides, some are vehicle-length-based
or magnetic-length-based methods which use more than
one sensor to implement vehicle classiﬁcation. This will
greatly increase the costs of system hardware and data
processing.
The main objective of this paper is to develop a more
effective vehicle classiﬁcation method by a single multi-
functional magnetic sensor for urban road. In this paper,
a novel method is proposed to classify vehicles by measur-
ing and processing ﬁeld magnetic signal based on a single
multi-functional sensor which is designed and developed.
We ﬁrstly proposed the on-line vehicle classiﬁcation
method with a single multi-functional magnetic sensor
and optimal MNS-based CART. The CART-based model
had a simple structure and fast execution efﬁciency. And
the multi-functional magnetic sensor was superior to the
existing sensors in terms of reliability, cost, size and func-
tion. Furthermore, by comparison with some current
methods, the proposed method was competitive in terms
of accuracy rate, sample robustness and execution time.
Finally, some experiments based on vehicle waveform data
in a ﬁeld road were implemented to test the performance
of the new method.

2. Data acquisition
The wireless multi-functional magnetic sensor
(referred to as ‘sensor’ in following texts) is used to
acquire the vehicle waveform data. The sensor is designed
and developed by our group ( Fig. 1 ). Its shape parameters
are diameter D = 100 mm and height H = 64 mm. The sen-
sor is capable of detecting real-time magnetic ﬁeld
strength. Making use of the magnetoresistance effect, it
translates the magnetic ﬁeld strength into mV level volt-
age signal, and then into V level voltage signal by an
ampliﬁer. A ﬁlter is used to reduce environment noise.
Then a digital output of the sensor will be obtained by
an A/D converter. The values of digital output are corre-
sponding to the variations that a vehicle disturbs the mag-
netic ﬁeld. If there is no vehicle around the sensor, the
digital output of the sensor would be an invariable value
(eliminating the stochastic noises), which is deﬁned as
the benchmark value y
b. When there is a vehicle passing
by the sensor, for the effect of ferromagnetic objects to
magnetic ﬁeld, the outputs of sensor are superposition
values of the benchmark value yb and the variations
caused by the vehicle, which is deﬁned as the vehicle
waveform. The multi-functional sensor can detect several
trafﬁc ﬂow parameters such as vehicle exist, vehicle stop,
vehicle retrogradation, vehicle classiﬁcation, trafﬁc count,
vehicle speed, occupancy ratios.
Due to the inﬂuences of environment and the sensor
itself, the vehicle waveform data contain some high fre-
quency noises (the sampling frequency fs = 333 Hz in this
paper). Therefore, a low-pass ﬁlter should be designed to
eliminate high frequency noises. By analyzing the fre-
quency distribution of noises, we designed a low-pass ﬁlter
and obtained smooth vehicle waveforms.
With a low-pass ﬁlter, the vehicle waveform will be
smoother and better for vehicle detection and feature
extraction. A dual-window vehicle detection algorithm is
used to extract the features of each vehicle waveform
[16]. A vehicle waveform of car is shown in Fig. 2 . yb is
the benchmark value. W1 and W2 are deﬁned as the vehicle
approach detection window (VADW) and the vehicle
departure detection window (VDDW), respectively.
The dual-window vehicle detection algorithm is
described as Algorithm 1 .

waveform features of each vehicle will be different, which
is the foundation for vehicle classiﬁcation.

implemented easily in sensor nodes with limited memory
and energy sources.
4. Methodology
4.1. Model and algorithm
Decision tree is a very efﬁcient machine learning classi-
ﬁcation model and it uses binary tree to represent process-
ing logic. The basic principle of a decision tree is that it
recursively splits the data into subsets so that each subset
contains a similar state or region of the data features.
When this recursive process is done, a decision tree is cre-
ated. Creating a decision tree is a process of tree growth
that ceaselessly splits data set. Each split is corresponding
to a decision problem, but also a node. For each split, a Max
‘difference’ between a subset and the rest of data set is
required [17].
A decision tree is made of decision nodes, branches and
leaves. The top node of decision nodes is the root node
and each branch generates a new decision node or a leaf.
Each decision node represents a problem or decision, and
generally corresponds to a feature of an object to be clas-
siﬁed. Each leaf node represents a possible classiﬁcation
result.
In this paper, the optimal MNS-based CART algorithm is
used to create a decision tree. The CART algorithm adopts a
binary recursive partitioning technology to split current
sample set into two subsets, which makes the resulting
decision tree has two branches for each non-leaf node.
The decision tree which is based on CART algorithm is a
binary tree with a simple structure [17]. The CART algo-
rithm is provided in Algorithm 2:

The algorithm parameter s is the MNS which relates to
the ﬁtting degree of training samples based decision tree
model. When s = 1, it will be the optimal division for the
training samples, but it will result in an over-ﬁtting deci-
sion tree. An over-ﬁtting decision tree is not appropriate
to classify new samples and will lead to low accuracy rates.
Therefore, it is necessary to ﬁnd an optimal s for CART algo-
rithm. Fortunately, the sample set is always limited and the
model learning is an off-line process. We can ﬁnd the opti-
mal s by computing the classiﬁcation accuracy rates of all
samples based on different decision trees trained with dif-
ferent values of s. The value of s corresponding to the max-
imum accuracy rate will be the optimal one.

CART can achieve self-learning according to the input
features, and can select the most effective features for
growing a decision tree. CART uses a ‘‘priors’’ mechanism.
Priors are akin to target class weights but they are invisible
in that they do not affect any counts reported by CART in
the tree. It automatically reweights the data to balance
the classes, and ensures that the tree selected as optimal

4.2. Off-line training and optimal MNS s
An optimal decision tree ( To) which is able to effectively
classify the known samples can be established by training
those samples. Then the on-line classiﬁcation process can
be executed by making use of the To. Denote m as the num-
ber of training samples, we can obtain the feature matrix
which is denoted as Xm/C2 8, that is Xm/C2 8 =[ x1, x2, ... , xi, ... ,
xm]T. Where xi is the feature vector of ith vehicle waveform
and xi =[ xi1, xi2, xi3, xi4, xi5, xi6, xi7, xi8], i =1 ,2 , ... , m. Denote yi
as the corresponding vehicle class of ith vehicle waveform
and the vehicle class vector will be y =[ y1, y2, ... , yi,... , ym]T.
By training sample feature matrix X m/C2 8 and corre-
sponding vehicle class vector y with different values of s,
a series of decision trees can be created. The decision tree
which has a maximum classiﬁcation accuracy rate of all
samples will be selected as the T
o, and corresponding value
of s is the optimal s. The structure of a decision tree can be
described as following. A triangle represents a decision
node which has two branches and a little dot represents
a leaf node which is a vehicle class.
4.3. Pruning
Due to possible over-ﬁtting for To, it will be necessary to
create an optimal pruning tree PTo by pruning a To some-
times. And then the PTo will be more robust to new sam-
ples. CART algorithm uses afterwards pruning method. By
pruning branches on an over-ﬁtting tree, the nodes which
are not pruned at the bottom will be denoted as leaf nodes.
This paper adopts a MEP-based rule to prune To and selects
a pruning tree with a maximum classiﬁcation accuracy rate
of the test samples as the optimal pruning tree PTo.
Making use of the To and PTo, it will be easy to imple-
ment a vehicle classiﬁcation algorithm in a sensor node
and to achieve on-line classiﬁcation for urban trafﬁc. In
the trees of To and PTo, the optimal features can be
revealed. In order to reduce computation, calculating those
optimal features will be enough for on-line vehicle classiﬁ-
cation in practice. On-line vehicle classiﬁcation is an ergo-
dic process along the decision tree from top to bottom. In
the process, it will go to next branch based on the decision
result of each decision node. The process will eventually
reach a leaf node. Reading the information of the leaf node,
a class of the new sample can be obtained.
5. Experiments
5.1. Vehicle samples
The magnetic sensors used for collecting vehicle wave-
form data in the paper are laid in the center of a lane of
Jiadadonglu road, Haidian district. From May, 2009 to
November, 2009, we collected vehicle waveform data of
470 cars and 72 busses, and used the video to record
the corresponding vehicle classes. Those 542 vehicle
waveforms were selected as sample sets to test our
method.
After two steps of vehicle waveform data acquisition
and feature extraction, the feature matrix of 542 vehicle
waveforms can be obtained (denoted as X542/C2 8). In order
to train and test different methods, 271 vehicle waveform
(including 235 cars and 36 busses) samples were selected
randomly as the training samples in this paper (denoted
as X
train). And the rest were test samples (denoted as
Xtest). At the same time, we used the videos to count the
actual vehicle classes. Denote 1 as the car, 2 as the bus,
and then we got the vehicle class vector ytrain correspond-
ing to Xtrain and vehicle class vector ytest corresponding to
Xtest.
5.2. Optimal MNS s
In order to obtain the optimal s, accuracy rates of
samples Xtrain and Xtest which are based on different deci-
sion trees with different values of s were computed
(Table 1 ). As shown in Table 1 , when s is small, the clas-
siﬁcation accuracy rate of Xtrain will be 100%, but the
accuracy rate and adaptability of Xtest are poor. As s
increases, the accuracy rate of Xtrain will fall; however,
the accuracy rate of Xtest can be improved. When s is
big enough, the decision tree will not be able to classify
the samples. In principle, s should not be larger than the
number of a class of least samples. Otherwise, it will
make against the classiﬁcation of a class of samples with
least number. In this paper, the averages of two accuracy
rates of Xtrain and Xtest will be computed with different s,
and the corresponding value of s with maximum average
rate will be the optimal one.
By computing the average classiﬁcation accuracy rates
of the sample set X542/C2 8, Fig. 5 shows the average accuracy
rates of Xtrain and Xtest which are based on decision trees
with different values of s. It can be drawn that when s is
4, 5, 6, 7, 8 or 9, we have the maximum average accuracy
rate. The greater the value of s is, the faster the speed of
algorithm converge will be, therefore, the optimal s is 9
in this paper.

6. Conclusions
Using self-developed multi-functional magnetic sen-
sors, the vehicle waveforms can be acquired in real time.
The magnetic sensor transmits data by radio, and it is con-
venient to deploy in urban roads. Besides, the sensor has a
low cost in hardware, maintenance and implementation.
Through two steps of vehicle waveform data processing
and feature extraction, the vehicle waveform features
which are suitable to vehicle classiﬁcation for urban road
can be obtained. Making use of those waveform features,
the MNS-based CART proposed in this paper enables efﬁ-
cient vehicle classiﬁcation. By pruning the optimal decision
tree based on MEP rule, the new optimal pruning tree can
effectively improve the accuracy and robustness for new
samples. For those do not need different features’ correla-
tion analysis, this method can give an optimal feature com-
bination by automatically ﬁltering and self-learning.
Compared with several current classiﬁcation methods,
the proposed method has obvious advantages in terms of
accuracy rate, sample robustness and execution time.
Moreover, comparing with traditional classiﬁcation
methods, the proposed method does not require vehicle
or magnetic length information, and achieves on-line
vehicle classiﬁcation by just a single multi-functional mag-
netic sensor. Therefore, this method also has advantages in
terms of cost and complexity of application system realiza-
tion. In addition, according to the multifarious application
requirements for vehicle classiﬁcation, we can train vari-
ous decision tree models to meet different applications of
vehicle classiﬁcation, which need to collect more samples
of different vehicle classes, and it is one of main directions
for future researches.
