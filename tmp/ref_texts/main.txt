abstract
This paper presents novel vehicle detection and classiﬁcation method by measuring and pro-
cessing magnetic signal based on single micro-electro-mechanical system (MEMS) magnetic
sensor. When a vehicle moves over the ground, it generates a succession of impacts on the
earth’s magnetic ﬁeld, which can be detected by single magnetic sensor. The magnetic signal
measured by the magnetic sensor is related to the moving direction and the type of the vehi-
cle. Generally, the recognition rate using single sensor detector is not high. In order to
improve the recognition rate, a novel feature extraction algorithm and a novel vehicle clas-
siﬁcation and recognition algorithm are presented. The concavity and convexity areas, and
the angles of concave and convex parts of the waveform are extracted. An improved support
vector machine (ISVM) classiﬁer is developed to perform vehicle classiﬁcation and recogni-
tion. The effectiveness of the proposed approach is veriﬁed by outdoor experiments.

1. Introduction
In recent years, Intelligent Transportation System (ITS)
programs have been initiated in many countries [1–3].A s
a part of ITS, moving vehicle detection and classiﬁcation
system plays an important role since it gathers all parts
of the data used in ITS. Video image, acoustic, seismic,
and infrared sensors are used in vehicle detection and clas-
siﬁcation systems. However, the detection and classiﬁca-
tion systems based on video image, acoustic, seismic and
infrared sensors are expensive and large in size due to
the usage of extra hardware. Furthermore, their perfor-
mance is often affected by Doppler effects and noise intro-
duced by moving vehicles and atmospheric and terrain
variations. In contrast, magnetic sensors are less sensitive
to noise and Doppler effects. Moreover, magnetic sensors
do not require the ‘line of sight’ assumption and are rela-
tively immune from weather conditions. They can detect
objects through obstacles such as foliage. Among various
magnetic sensors, the micro-electro-mechanical system
(MEMS) magnetic sensors have advantages in terms of
cost, size, weight and power consumption [4–7]. Hence,
applying MEMS magnetic sensors to vehicle detection,
classiﬁcation and recognition is a promising research topic.
Currently, some methods on vehicle detection and clas-
siﬁcation using magnetic sensors have been reported in lit-
erature. These methods are either based on the fusion of
magnetic and other kinds of sensing techniques or based
on the fusion of magnetic arrays. Ding et al. proposed a
vehicle detection algorithm called the Adaptive Threshold
algorithm based on acoustic sensor and magnetic sensor
[8].I n [9], Casalegno proposed a vehicle classiﬁcation
method based on magnetometer arrays. Their recognition
rates are between 80% and 90%. Since multiple sensors
and fusion techniques are used, these methods are usually
complicated.
In this paper, a new approach is proposed to detect and
classify vehicles by measuring and processing magnetic
signal based on single MEMS sensor. It is superior to the
existing methods in terms of reliability, installation and
maintenance. The feasibility of the approach is based on
the magnetic signal which is closely related to the

2. Foundation of vehicle detection and recognition
using single magnetic sensor
The sensor considered in this paper is the 1-axis MEMS
magnetic sensor from Honeywell [10]. It is made of Perm-
alloy (NiFe) thin ﬁlm deposited onto a silicon substrate and
patterned to form a Wheatstone resistor bridge (see Fig. 1)
which can generate a differential output voltage. In the
bridge, the opposite elements are identical. If the magnetic
ﬁeld in the sensitive axis increases, the Vb to Out+ and
Out/C0 to GND elements will slightly decrease in resistance
while the other two elements will increase in resistance.
This results in an increase of voltage at Out+ and a decrease
of voltage at Out /C0 , or equivalently an increase of voltage
from Out+ to Out /C0 . Similarly, if the magnetic ﬁeld in the
sensitive axis decreases, the voltage across Out+ and Out /C0
will decrease. Hence, by measuring the voltage across Out+
and Out/C0 , one can detect the change of the magnetic ﬁeld
in which the sensor is placed.
The magnetic ﬁeld disturbance produced by a moving
vehicle can be measured by a single axis magnetic sensor.
The distortion in the earth’s magnetic ﬁeld concentrates
mainly at locations near the engine and wheels. However,
it can also vary depending on the internal distribution of
the ferrous materials used in other parts of the vehicle, such
as rooftop and trunk locations. The net result is a character-
istic distortion to the earth’s magnetic ﬁeld that is unique to
the shape of the vehicle. To verify this view point, we used a
MEMS magnetic sensor to detect typical vehicle targets. The
measurement system is shown in Fig. 2 which includes a
power unit, a MEMS magnetic sensor and conditioning cir-
cuit, a high-speed A/D–D/A data acquisition card and a com-
puter. The sensor was installed by the side of a road and the
vehicles ran beside it (see Fig. 3).
The test was carried out on a straight concrete road that
is 2-km long and 12-m wide. The vehicles tested include
heavy tracked vehicles, light tracked vehicles and light
wheeled vehicles. It was a sunny day with 30–35 /C176C
temperature and 1–2 wind force level. The sensor is in-
stalled in the way that its sensitive axis is parallel with
the directions of moving vehicles. The direction of sensitive
axis of the sensor is called ‘‘forward direction” and the re-
verse direction of sensitive axis is called ‘‘reverse direc-
tion”. The speeds of target vehicles were not the same
but each vehicle passed by the sensor with a uniform
speed. Also, the vehicles moved passed the sensor within
a distance of three meters to ensure that the vehicles were
in the detection range of the sensor. The sensor signal was
sampled at the frequency of 2000 Hz.
Fig. 4 shows the signal waveforms generated by a heavy
tracked vehicle traveling in different directions. It can be
seen from Fig. 4b that when there is no vehicle in the sen-
sor’s detection range, the output of the sensing circuit is
initially zero. When the vehicle is traveling in forward
direction, the output will ﬁrst decrease below the initial
value. Then it increases and reaches the initial value at
the time when the vehicle is in front of the sensor. When
the vehicle moves further, the ﬂux lines will bend toward
the vehicle. As a result, the signal value increases and be-
comes positive. It will return to the initial value again
when the vehicle moves out of the sensor’s detection
range. Fig. 4a shows the signal waveform when the vehicle
travels in the reverse direction. It is easy to see that the
waveform in Fig. 4 a is approximately a mirror reﬂection

3. Vehicle recognition based on the features of magnetic
signals
The vehicle recognition process is shown in Fig. 6 .I t
consists of four steps: noise elimination, signal segmenta-
tion, feature extraction, and vehicle classiﬁcation and
recognition.
3.1. Noise elimination
In some cases, relatively strong additive noise appears
and affects the measurements. The noise signals could be
caused by environmental factors and/or by the operations
of electronic circuits, such as the data transmission from
the acquisition card to the host computer. The spectrum
analysis shows that the periods of the noise signals
encountered in this application are less than several micro
seconds. In contrast, as shown in Figs. 4 and 5 , the length of
a vehicle signal waveform is much greater than the periods
of noise signals. As a result, a low-pass ﬁlter can be used to
effectively remove noise signals. Here we use the digital ﬁl-
tering technique to eliminate noise [11–14]. The effective-
ness of this technique can be demonstrated by the example
shown in Fig. 7 . It is clear that after noise elimination, the
useful information of the magnetic signal is well reserved.
This technique is applied to ﬁltering magnetic signals gen-
erated by various vehicles and satisfactory results are ob-
tained in all cases.

3.4. Vehicle classiﬁcation and recognition
The support vector machine (SVM) is a statistical learn-
ing method based on the structure risk minimization prin-
ciple. Its efﬁciency has been proved in many pattern
recognition applications and can be used to classify and
recognize vehicles. To classify more than two types of vehi-
cles, a multi-class SVM must be used. The two widely used
multi-class SVMs are the one-against-all method and the
one-against-one method [15–17]. In the one-against-all
method, a set of binary classiﬁers are ﬁrst trained to be
able to separate each class from the others. Then each data
object is classiﬁed to the class for which the largest deci-
sion value was determined. To classify M classes, this
method needs M classiﬁers. Differently, in the
one-against-one method, a series of classiﬁers is applied
to each pair of classes, with the most commonly computed
class kept for each object. Then a max-win operator is used
to determine to which class the object will be ﬁnally as-
signed. This method requires

4. Experimental results and analysis
4.1. Experimental results
The experiments were conducted on a straight road that
is 2-km long and 12-m wide. The surface of the road is
reinforced concrete. The sensor was properly installed to
ensure that its sensitive axis was parallel with the direc-
tions of moving vehicles. The proposed vehicle detection
and classiﬁcation approach was tested using 93 vehicles,
including 27 heavy tracked vehicles, 28 light tracked vehi-
cles and 38 light wheeled vehicles. The vehicles passed the
sensor within a distance of three meters to ensure that the
vehicles were in the detection range of the sensor. The sen-
sor signal was sampled at the frequency of 2000 Hz. The
structure of the detection system and the sensor installa-
tion diagram are shown in Figs. 2 and 3 , respectively. Dur-
ing the test period, the weather was ﬁne, with 30–35 /C176C
temperature and 1–2 wind force level.
In the experiments, the features of concavity area, con-
vexity area, the angle of concave part, and the angle of con-
vex part of the waveform are extracted for vehicle
detection and classiﬁcation. The successful vehicle detec-
tion rate is 100%. Then the ISVM classiﬁer which is based
on the improved one-against-all algorithm is used to clas-
sify and recognize the vehicles. The results of vehicle
detection and recognition using the ISVM classiﬁer are
shown in Table 1 . Given the same extracted features, we
also use other classiﬁers to classify and recognize vehicles,
including the support vector machine (SVM), the back-
propagation neural networks (BPNN) and the k-nearest
neighbor (KNN) classiﬁers. The recognition rates on these
classiﬁers are shown in Table 2 .
4.2. Experimental analysis
Due to complex environment and some uncertain inter-
ference, errorless recognition cannot be guaranteed at this
stage. We can see from Table 1 that the recognition rates of
the proposed ISVM classiﬁer are higher than 92% for the
heavy tracked vehicles and light tracked vehicles, while
its recognition rate on the light wheeled vehicles is
89.5%. The relatively lower recognition rate on the light
wheeled vehicle result from the smaller size and volume

5. Conclusion
This paper shows that the signal measured by a MEMS
magnetic sensor contains useful features of the vehicle
passing by the sensor. By exploiting the features of the
magnetic signal, a novel approach is proposed to detect,
classify and recognize the moving vehicles. This approach
is simple as only one magnetic sensor is used and efﬁcient
in computation thanks to the usage of the ISVM classiﬁer
which is based on an improved one-against-all algorithm.
Our experiments show that the recognition rate of the pro-
posed approach is about 90%. Compared with the commer-
cial vehicle detection devices, our experimental system can
not only detect the passage of vehicles but also classify and
recognize the types of vehicles with high recognition rate.
